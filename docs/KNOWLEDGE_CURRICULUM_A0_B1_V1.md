# LINGUA OS — A0–B1 Knowledge Curriculum Blueprint V1

Status: research-backed curriculum hypothesis, not runtime data  
Audience: Knowledge importer/content authoring/review  
Scope: Vietnamese learners of English, owner self-use first

## 1. Curriculum philosophy

LINGUA OS should not reduce “knowledge” to a graded word list. A useful A0–B1 knowledge system combines:

- high-value lexical concepts;
- phrase/formulaic concepts;
- grammar/construction concepts;
- communicative-function skills;
- discourse/connective knowledge;
- context-rich examples;
- future phonological knowledge focused on intelligibility;
- provenance and level evidence.

The learner model remains separate. This blueprint describes what may be worth teaching, not what a learner knows.

## 2. CEFR alignment stance

The Council of Europe CEFR describes progressive communicative ability rather than language-specific word inventories. It is useful as the top-level capability frame. English-specific resources such as CEFR-J and the British Council/Eaquals Core Inventory are evidence for content selection, not infallible truth.

A British Council validation study of the Core Inventory found that many proposed level assignments were uncertain rather than cleanly confirmed. LINGUA OS should therefore preserve source/confidence/review evidence rather than hard-code every teaching point as “the official CEFR level.”

The Companion Volume also adds Pre-A1, mediation, richer listening/reading descriptors and a revised phonology model. Future pronunciation work should optimize intelligibility, sound articulation and prosody rather than imitation of a native accent.

## 3. Four curriculum bands

The bands below are product curriculum hypotheses to be checked against CEFR-J source rows and real content. They are not copied CEFR descriptors and not certification claims.

### Band 0 — Pre-A1 / LINGUA A0 bootstrap

Purpose: make a complete beginner functional enough to understand the product and survive tiny, highly supported English exchanges.

#### Lexical domains

Prioritize a very small, extremely reusable set:

- self/people: name, person, family, friend;
- basic actions: be, have, go, come, want, need, like, know, help;
- concrete daily nouns: home, room, food, water, phone, bus, school/work;
- numbers and very basic time/day expressions;
- high-value adjectives: good/bad, big/small, hot/cold, happy/tired;
- basic location/direction: here, there, in, on, near;
- basic question/response words.

This set is first-party curriculum scaffolding because CEFR-J vocabulary begins at A1.

#### Phrase/function concepts

Examples of functions to support with original phrases, not fixed copied lists:

- greeting and leave-taking;
- stating/asking a name;
- saying yes/no/not understanding;
- asking someone to repeat or slow down;
- asking for help;
- expressing a very basic need/want;
- thanking/apologizing;
- asking what/where/who in highly supported contexts.

#### Grammar/construction candidates

- basic subject pronouns;
- core `be` statements/questions/negation;
- simple noun phrases;
- demonstratives;
- basic possession;
- highly frequent imperatives;
- basic `can`/`can't` for ability/request where useful;
- basic singular/plural awareness.

All exact level placement must be validated before catalog import.

#### Content behavior

- extremely short sentences;
- one main proposition per sentence;
- high repetition but varied micro-contexts;
- Vietnamese support readily available;
- avoid rare synonyms and decorative vocabulary.

### Band 1 — A1 everyday foundation

Purpose: operate in familiar everyday situations with simple language and build enough vocabulary/grammar for short Reader content.

#### Lexical domains

- personal information and relationships;
- daily routine and household activities;
- food/drink and simple ordering;
- shopping, prices and quantities;
- local places and directions;
- transport and basic travel;
- weather and clothing;
- leisure and preferences;
- basic study/work vocabulary;
- common feelings and physical states.

#### Phrase/function concepts

- likes/dislikes;
- asking/giving simple directions;
- ordering/buying;
- asking time/price/quantity;
- invitations and simple arrangements;
- routine requests;
- asking/giving simple personal information;
- basic clarification and repair;
- short reason frames such as simple `because` patterns.

#### Grammar/construction candidates

- present simple statements/questions/negation;
- frequency expressions;
- `there is/there are`;
- articles/determiners and core countability distinctions;
- possessives;
- basic preposition patterns;
- present continuous for obvious current activity;
- common modal/imperative patterns;
- simple past introduction where source/content evidence supports it.

#### Discourse

- simple coordination (`and`, `but`, basic `because`);
- short ordered sequences;
- pronoun reference over two or three sentences.

### Band 2 — A2 routine independence

Purpose: handle common routine tasks, talk about background and experiences in simple terms, and read short connected content with manageable support.

#### Lexical domains

Expand depth rather than just breadth:

- travel and accommodation;
- health and appointments;
- education/work routines;
- services and everyday problems;
- hobbies/media/technology;
- personal history and experiences;
- plans and goals;
- comparing places/products/activities;
- environment/community at a concrete level.

#### Phrase/function concepts

- describing past events;
- making/changing plans;
- simple advice and obligation;
- comparison and preference;
- explaining a simple problem;
- asking for more detail;
- offering/suggesting;
- agreeing/disagreeing simply;
- giving short reasons and consequences.

#### Grammar/construction candidates

- past simple system and common irregulars;
- present vs present-continuous contrast;
- future plans/predictions using high-frequency constructions;
- comparatives/superlatives;
- quantifiers/countability expansion;
- modal advice/obligation/possibility basics;
- infinitive/gerund high-frequency frames;
- present perfect core experience/relevance uses where evidence supports placement;
- basic conditionals and relative-clause introductions where content requires them.

#### Discourse

- time-order connectors;
- reason/result connectors;
- paragraph-level reference and repetition control;
- short narrative arc: situation -> event -> result.

### Band 3 — B1 connected communication

Purpose: understand the main points of clear familiar input, handle most everyday travel/problem situations and produce connected language about familiar interests, experiences, opinions and plans.

#### Lexical domains

- richer work/study and personal development;
- travel problems and solutions;
- relationships and social situations;
- news/current-life topics at a familiar, non-specialist level;
- technology and online life;
- health/lifestyle habits;
- city/community/environment decisions;
- goals, ambitions and decision-making;
- emotions/opinions beyond basic polarity.

#### Phrase/function concepts

- stating and supporting an opinion;
- agreeing/disagreeing politely;
- comparing alternatives and trade-offs;
- explaining causes, results and plans;
- narrating a sequence with background/detail;
- giving advice and recommendations;
- handling misunderstandings;
- summarizing the main point of familiar material;
- reporting what another person said at a simple level;
- making a complaint/request with basic register control.

#### Grammar/construction candidates

- present-perfect/past contrasts in common contexts;
- first conditional and other high-frequency conditional patterns;
- relative clauses;
- passive constructions where frequent/useful;
- modal nuance for obligation/possibility/advice;
- indirect questions and polite request forms;
- reported speech core patterns;
- wider clause combining and subordination;
- more controlled article/preposition/collocation patterns.

#### Discourse/pragmatics

- paragraph cohesion;
- sequencing/backgrounding;
- contrast/concession at a basic independent-user level;
- stance and hedging with common phrases;
- register awareness for familiar service/work/study contexts.

## 4. Cross-level knowledge domains

### 4.1 Lexical breadth vs depth

For early A0/A1, prioritize breadth of very frequent/utilitarian concepts. As the learner moves toward A2/B1, increase depth:

- additional common senses;
- collocations;
- phrase frames;
- word-family awareness;
- register distinctions;
- more varied context.

Do not equate “more dictionary senses” with progression.

### 4.2 Formulaic language

Phrase concepts should grow through all levels. Early levels need survival chunks; later levels need collocations, phrasal verbs, stance/discourse frames and polite interaction patterns.

A phrase can be valuable even when every component word is already known.

### 4.3 Grammar as usage patterns

Grammar concepts should be tied to examples/functions rather than long abstract rules. A construction should enter the core catalog because it helps understand/produce real content, not because a textbook table contains it.

### 4.4 Sociolinguistic/pragmatic knowledge

At A0/A1 this may be simple politeness and safe forms of address. By B1, the learner benefits from basic register, softening, turn-taking and context-appropriate request/complaint patterns.

Do not model these as individual learner competence dimensions until an accepted domain decision exists.

### 4.5 Mediation

The CEFR Companion Volume treats mediation as an important communication mode. For MVP, LINGUA OS need not add a new learner-state dimension. Knowledge/content can still prepare for future activities such as:

- relaying the main point of a simple message;
- explaining a familiar idea more simply;
- summarizing short familiar information.

Keep this as activity-design intelligence only for now.

## 5. Vietnamese-specific review lens

Every candidate does **not** need a “Vietnamese difficulty” tag. Instead, apply an authoring/review checklist when the item overlaps recurring L1 transfer risks.

### Grammar/lexis review prompts

- Does article/countability usage differ materially from a natural Vietnamese mapping?
- Is a preposition choice likely to be translated literally?
- Is tense/aspect meaning clearer if contrasted with Vietnamese time-marking strategies?
- Is a collocation likely to invite a plausible but non-native combination?
- Are near-synonyms distinguishable in simple Vietnamese?

### Future phonology review prompts

When pronunciation becomes a bounded product feature:

- prioritize intelligibility over accent imitation;
- review final consonants/clusters;
- review selected English contrasts often difficult for Vietnamese speakers;
- include word stress/prosody, not isolated phonemes only;
- never label a learner weak from L1 background alone.

## 6. Prerequisite-graph seed policy

A prerequisite edge is stronger than “related” and should remain sparse.

Candidate dependency shapes to review:

- basic pronoun + `be` -> richer predication patterns;
- simple noun phrase/determiner awareness -> countability/article expansion;
- present-simple question structure -> later indirect questions;
- present simple -> frequency/routine discourse;
- past simple -> connected narrative sequencing;
- simple clause connectors -> B1 connected explanation;
- core modals -> advice/obligation nuance;
- comparison structures -> expressing evaluated preferences/trade-offs.

These are candidate pedagogical dependencies, not automatic graph imports. A reviewer must justify each persisted `prerequisite` edge.

## 7. Context diversity ladder

Examples should become more diverse as knowledge stabilizes:

1. **Introduction:** one highly transparent context.
2. **Disambiguation:** another context that confirms the same core meaning/use.
3. **Transfer:** different topic/syntax while preserving target sense.
4. **Productive frame:** context that supports constrained learner production.
5. **Authentic Reader exposure:** real LINGUA content encounter when rights/quality fit.

This ladder guides content authoring only. Number of contexts is not a competence score.

## 8. First 16 micro-content coverage plan

Do not author the articles in this knowledge branch. Use four content archetypes per band to discover catalog gaps without colliding with parallel content/UI work.

### A0 archetypes

- self + morning routine;
- room/home objects and location;
- tiny cafe/order exchange;
- asking for help/clarification.

### A1 archetypes

- local transport/directions;
- shopping/food quantities;
- weekend plan/preferences;
- meeting a new classmate/colleague.

### A2 archetypes

- a past travel problem;
- an appointment/health routine;
- comparing two places/options;
- planning a short trip or event.

### B1 archetypes

- choosing between study/work options;
- online habits and a simple viewpoint;
- a neighborhood/community problem and proposed solution;
- an experience narrative with reasons/lessons/plans.

The future missing-concept report from these archetypes should determine catalog growth. Do not pre-fill to an arbitrary number.

## 9. Content difficulty model — evidence to collect, not constants to invent

Research on CEFR-J text profiling and lexical-complexity tools suggests text level depends on multiple features, not one word list or sentence length. For each real article, a future preview report should capture:

- distribution of mapped lexical levels;
- unknown/unmapped lexical items;
- target-new concept density;
- average/maximum sentence length;
- clause/verb density or another deterministic syntax proxy;
- phrase/grammar target density;
- learner-known and learner-learning coverage;
- support available (gloss/Vietnamese/context);
- content mode: reading vs listening, because listening texts may need different complexity expectations.

Do **not** hard-code 95%, 98% or another lexical-coverage threshold as a mastery/content gate before owner self-use data validates the UX.

## 10. Curriculum QA questions

Before approving a band/pack, reviewer asks:

1. Does each concept support a real learner action, comprehension need or article?
2. Are phrase concepts sufficiently represented, or is the pack only isolated words?
3. Are grammar concepts attached to concrete use/functions?
4. Are Vietnamese explanations concise and natural?
5. Are likely L1-transfer traps explained only where useful, without stereotyping the learner?
6. Does content provide varied contexts without accidentally creating fake evidence?
7. Are level assignments traced to sources/review rather than asserted as official truth?
8. Are prerequisite edges sparse and justified?
9. Can the pack be learned through recognition/listening/production activities without adding a second mastery model?
10. Does the pack improve the first real Reader loop more than simply importing another thousand dictionary entries?
